# Blue Valley Acres Website Design Document

## Brand strategy, site structure, visual direction and launch copy

**Project:** bluevalleyacres.co  
**Prepared:** October 29, 2026  
**Purpose:** A strategic and creative brief for planning and prompting the future Blue Valley Acres website. This document defines direction and content; it is not a coded website.

> **Creative premise**  
> Blue Valley Acres is a real Colorado community with a remarkable setting and a neighborly spirit. The website should make both visible: the landscape draws people in, and clear, current community information helps them feel at home.

## 1. Executive direction

Build a warm, confident community website that feels connected to the land without borrowing the visual language of a luxury ranch, resort, or real estate listing. Blue Valley Acres should come across as a lived-in place: open country, mountain weather, the Blue River nearby, practical stewardship, and people who look out for one another.

The site has two equally important jobs. First, it should introduce the community to prospective residents and visitors with strong photography, a clear sense of place, and accurate information. Second, it should help current members quickly find documents, meeting information, river access guidance, safety notices, and the right organization to contact.

The experience should be visually distinctive but easy to use on a phone. Keep the public story welcoming and concise. Put time-sensitive notices and member resources in predictable places. Separate Land Share information from Metropolitan District services and link between them clearly.

### Core positioning

**Blue Valley Acres is a close-knit Colorado community along the Blue River, set between Kremmling and Silverthorne, where wide-open mountain country and everyday neighborliness belong together.**

### Selected tagline system

**Primary tagline:** Where mountain, river, and community meet.

**Supporting line:** A shared place.

**Stewardship message:** Land worth caring for.

### Descriptive homepage lead

**A Colorado community along the Blue River.**

### Supporting narrative

**Set between Kremmling and Silverthorne, Blue Valley Acres brings neighbors together in a beautiful stretch of mountain country shaped by open skies, river country and a shared respect for the place we call home.**

### Brand promise

A beautiful place to live, with the practical information and neighborly connection that make it feel like home.

### What the site should communicate

- A genuine residential community, not a resort or gated luxury development.
- A striking setting along the Highway 9 corridor between Kremmling and Silverthorne.
- Shared stewardship of roads, common areas, river access and community life, with responsibilities described accurately.
- A welcoming community voice that respects both long-time residents and new neighbors.
- Useful, current information that is easy to find on desktop and mobile.

## 2. Audience and user needs

- **Current residents and Land Share members:** Notices, meeting dates, governing documents, maps, contacts, community updates and the annual dues payment page. Next step: Open Member Resources or the relevant notice.
- **New residents:** A clear welcome, who manages what, how to get oriented, and where to ask questions. Next step: Visit New Here and contact the right organization.
- **Prospective residents and property owners:** A factual overview of setting, community character, access and nearby towns. Next step: Explore the Community and view the photo gallery.
- **Guests and family:** Directions, local safety guidance and river access rules. Next step: Read Visit and Safety Information.
- **Local agencies and service partners:** Accurate contact details and clear organizational boundaries. Next step: Use the contact directory.

Design for the common mobile visit first: a resident receives a notice link, opens it on a phone, and needs the latest information or contact in seconds. Keep phone links, document links, dates and labels legible and tappable.

## 3. Brand character and voice

### Character

- **Rooted:** The landscape is specific to this place, not generic mountain scenery.
- **Neighborly:** Speak with warmth and shared responsibility, not corporate formality.
- **Capable:** Community information is organized, current and direct.
- **Open:** Welcome newcomers while respecting community norms and private property.
- **Unpretentious:** Let the setting and real people carry the story.

### Voice rules

Write in plain, human language. Prefer “neighbors,” “community,” and “the place we share” over marketing phrases. Be proud without claiming that Blue Valley Acres is the best, most exclusive, or only place of its kind. Use specific facts where they have been confirmed. Date notices and identify who issued them. Explain terms such as Land Share, Metropolitan District, common area and river access in everyday language.

Avoid: “luxury living,” “exclusive enclave,” “untouched wilderness,” unsupported superlatives, resort language, and claims that imply every resident has the same services or access. Avoid promising public access, guaranteed fishing, year-round road conditions, or utility availability without confirming governing documents and current conditions.

### Sample brand language

- “A good place to put down roots.”
- “A community shaped by the land around it.”
- “Good neighbors. Big country. Room to breathe.”
- “Welcome to the neighborhood.”

Use these as optional supporting copy. The selected tagline system is “Where mountain, river, and community meet” as the primary tagline, “A shared place” as a concise supporting line, and “Land worth caring for” as the stewardship message. Use “A Colorado community along the Blue River” when a concise geographic description is useful.

## 4. Visual identity direction

### Design idea: Modern mountain community

Use an editorial, nature-led layout with generous whitespace, strong landscape photography, calm typography and restrained details inspired by maps and field notes. The result should feel polished and contemporary, while still belonging to a practical rural community. Do not overuse wood textures, rope, badges, distressed type, antlers, or western clichés.

### Color palette

- **River Slate `#315C68`:** Primary links, buttons and river-inspired accents.
- **Deep Spruce `#263B35`:** Main dark background, footer and high-contrast type.
- **Sage `#879586`:** Secondary accent, tags and subtle graphic elements.
- **Meadow Gold `#C7A96B`:** Small highlights, dividers and active states.
- **Warm Stone `#E9E4D9`:** Section backgrounds and card surfaces.
- **Cloud Ivory `#F7F6F1`:** Main page background.
- **Charcoal `#242A28`:** Body copy and headings on light backgrounds.
- **White `#FFFFFF`:** Cards, reverse text and clear negative space.

Use River Slate and Deep Spruce for strong, readable text and controls. Reserve Meadow Gold for small accents rather than body text. Maintain WCAG AA contrast for all essential text and controls; verify actual combinations during implementation.

### Typography

**Recommended display typeface: Newsreader** (Google Fonts). Use for large headlines, pull quotes and occasional editorial statements. It brings a quiet, literary character without feeling formal.

**Recommended interface and body typeface: DM Sans** (Google Fonts). Use for paragraphs, navigation, forms, buttons, labels and utility content. Its clear shapes remain readable at small mobile sizes.

Set display headings with moderate weight and tight but comfortable leading. Keep body copy at 17–19 px on desktop and at least 16 px on mobile, with a line height around 1.55. Use sentence case for navigation and buttons. Limit uppercase to small utility labels.

**Fallbacks:** `Georgia, serif` for Newsreader; `Arial, sans-serif` for DM Sans. Load fonts efficiently and ensure the page remains readable before web fonts finish loading.

### Photography and media

Photography is the main visual asset. Prioritize authentic, locally made images over stock. Build a seasonal library with wide landscape frames, details of the river and vegetation, roads and homes in context, community gatherings, and candid portraits made with permission.

- Hero: a wide, true-to-place view that shows the scale and character of the area. Do not imply the entire community sits on riverfront property.
- Community: real homes and neighborhood roads photographed respectfully, without exposing private details.
- Shared places: river access or common areas only where use and ownership are verified.
- People: neighbors at a gathering, working on a shared project, or enjoying the setting. Obtain written permission before publishing identifiable people.
- Seasons: use summer and fall as primary palette references; include winter and spring to show the year-round character.
- Video: optional short, quiet landscape film with natural sound. No autoplay with sound. Provide captions and a still-image fallback.

Image treatment should be natural, color-true and lightly warm. Avoid heavy teal/orange grading, excessive drone footage, fake HDR and generic resort imagery. Add descriptive alternative text to informative images; decorative backgrounds should not repeat the same information.

## 7. Page copy and content recommendations

### About Blue Valley Acres

**A community with room to breathe.**

Blue Valley Acres is a residential community in Grand County, Colorado, along Highway 9 between Kremmling and Silverthorne. Its setting brings together broad mountain views, open country and a close connection to the Blue River corridor. What makes the community special is the combination of that landscape and the neighbors who care for this place together.

The community includes different filings and service arrangements. For current details about a particular property, contact the appropriate organization and review the governing documents. The Land Share and the Blue Valley Metropolitan District have different responsibilities; this site will help you find the right information.

**Our shared place**  
Roads, common areas and community resources work best when residents understand how they are maintained and how to take part. Find current documents and notices in Member Resources.

### New Resident Guide

**Welcome to Blue Valley Acres.**

We are glad you are here. Getting settled is easier when you know where to find the right information. Start with these steps:

1. Confirm which Blue Valley Acres filing your property is in and review the documents that apply to it.
2. Learn whether your question belongs with Blue Valley Acres Land Share Inc. or the Blue Valley Metropolitan District.
3. Review current notices, meeting information and contact details before reaching out.
4. Read the community's safety and stewardship guidance, including current fire restrictions and wildlife recommendations.
5. Ask a neighbor or use the Contact page if you are unsure where to begin.

**Who manages what?**  
Blue Valley Acres Land Share Inc. and the Blue Valley Metropolitan District are separate organizations. Their responsibilities, documents and contacts should be confirmed with each organization and shown side by side on this page. Link directly to the District's official website for water, payments, governance, architectural review and District notices where applicable.

### Member Resources

**Useful information, in one place.**

Find current Land Share documents, maps, notices and community contacts here. Each item should show its title, issuing organization, date or version, and whether it is public or member-only. For District services and records, visit the Blue Valley Metropolitan District website.

Suggested categories: Pay Annual Landowner Dues; Governing Documents; Maps and Common Areas; Meetings and Notices; River Access and Fishing; Safety and Stewardship; District Services.

### Pay Annual Landowner Dues

**Pay your annual landowner dues.**

Make your annual dues payment securely online. Enter your parcel number and owner information so we can credit the payment to the correct property.

**Annual dues: $400 per property.** The payment is a one-time annual charge. The page should state that the dues support roads, community areas and care for the place we share. Dues are for Blue Valley Acres Land Share; District water bills and other District charges belong on the Blue Valley Metropolitan District website.

**Owner information**

- Parcel Number (required; shown first)
- Owner Name (required)
- Email Address (required, for the receipt and payment status)
- Phone Number (optional)

**Choose a payment method**

The current checkout concept offers Bank Transfer (ACH) and Credit / Debit Card. The payer covers the processing fee, shown as a separate line before payment authorization. Based on the current $400 design reference, the example totals are:

- **Bank Transfer (ACH):** $400.00 dues + $3.20 processing fee (0.8%) = **$403.20**
- **Credit / Debit Card:** $400.00 dues + $11.90 processing fee (2.9% + $0.30) = **$411.90**

Treat these fee values as the current checkout design figures and verify the active provider fee schedule before launch. Keep the amount due, fee and final total visible after a payment method is selected.

**Optional community support**

Offer one optional contribution choice, with neither selected by default:

- Road maintenance and snow-plow fund
- River and erosion fund

Allow the owner to enter an optional dollar amount. Keep the contribution separate from required dues, identify the selected fund in the payment record and receipt, and update the total before the owner continues.

**Primary button:** Continue to Payment  
**Security line:** Secure checkout powered by Stripe  
**Confirmation:** After payment, show a confirmation with the parcel number, dues amount, selected payment method, fee, optional contribution, total and transaction reference. Email a receipt to the owner. For ACH, distinguish “Payment pending” from “Payment complete”; update the ledger only after the provider reports the payment as successful.

### Page layout and behavior

Use the supplied checkout concept as the visual reference: scenic Blue Valley header; clear “Annual Landowner Dues” title; owner information card; $400 dues summary; two payment-method cards with itemized totals; optional support selector; and a full-width Continue to Payment button. Keep the form calm, mobile-friendly and consistent with the site palette. On mobile, stack the payment cards and keep the selected method, processing fee and total together.

Build the public-facing page in Wix at `/pay-dues`, with a persistent **Pay Dues** entry under Member Resources and a visible site header button. The payment page should be easy for landowners to open without requiring a website account unless the board explicitly requires login.

Use a Wix payment form or checkout configuration connected to Stripe if it can collect the parcel and owner fields, itemize the chosen fee, preserve optional fund data and return a useful confirmation. Wix Forms supports fixed-price, custom-price and donation fields; if a single native Wix flow cannot combine the required ACH method, fee display and optional contribution in one transaction, use a custom Wix/Velo front end that creates one provider checkout transaction. Keep the site page and flow in Wix.

Before launch, connect and verify the actual Wix payment account. Wix's available payment methods vary by country and provider; confirm ACH Direct Debit is available in the specific account and checkout flow. Wix notes that ACH status can take up to four business days to settle. Do not represent pending ACH as paid. Confirm the applicable provider and payment-network rules for passing fees to the owner, and show the full fee and total before authorization. Never store raw bank or card credentials in the Wix form or site database.

### Location and Getting Here

**Easy to find. A world away from the everyday.**

Blue Valley Acres is located on Highway 9 between Kremmling and Silverthorne in Grand County, Colorado. Use the interactive map for general orientation and follow local road and weather conditions before traveling. Exact distances and drive times should be verified before publication and should be presented as estimates.

Use a map that identifies the community entrance and public roads without disclosing private driveways, individual homes or sensitive infrastructure. Add accessible written directions alongside the map.

### Safety and Stewardship

**Take care on the land. Look out for one another.**

Mountain weather, wildlife and changing conditions are part of life here. Check official current guidance before heading out, keep a respectful distance from wildlife, secure food and trash, and follow posted fire restrictions. Notices on this site should identify the source and date, and should link to the responsible agency for current rules.

Do not present old alerts as current. For an emergency, contact emergency services directly; this website is not an emergency response channel.

### Contact

**How can we help you find the right information?**

Send a general question and we will point you to the right resource. For water service, billing, governance, architectural review or District matters, contact the Blue Valley Metropolitan District directly. For Land Share documents and community matters, use the verified Land Share contact.

Form fields: Name; Email; Topic; Message. Include a short privacy note and an expected response window only if the organization can meet it. Add a spam-protected form and a visible email alternative.

## 8. Interaction and UX direction

The site should feel calm and responsive. Use small, purposeful motion to support orientation rather than entertain at the expense of usability.

- Sticky or compacting navigation that preserves the logo and primary Member Resources link.
- A mobile menu with large touch targets, visible focus states and clear section labels.
- Gentle hero image movement only if it does not reduce text legibility or affect performance. Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Card hover states with a slight lift or border shift; retain an equivalent visible focus state for keyboard users.
- Photo gallery with simple category filters, keyboard navigation and captions. Do not trap users in a lightbox.
- Notice cards with plain status labels such as “Current notice,” issuing body and date. Never depend on color alone to signal urgency.
- Searchable document list only if document volume justifies it; otherwise use a clean categorized library.
- Dues payment: parcel number first; required name and email; optional phone; ACH and card choices; visible fee and changing total; optional support choices for one fund at a time; confirmation and receipt. Distinguish pending ACH from completed payment.
- Interactive map with an accessible static map and written directions fallback.
- Optional seasonal image transition on the homepage, but never rotate or change content so quickly that visitors cannot read it.

## 9. Content operations and governance

A useful community site depends on a dependable update process. Before launch, name the people who can approve, publish and archive information. Every notice should have an owner, publish date, review date and expiration/removal rule.

### Suggested ownership model

- **Land Share editor:** community pages, Land Share documents, common-area updates and Land Share notices.
- **District liaison:** verified links and summaries of District services and public notices; District source remains authoritative for District business.
- **Site administrator:** access, backups, form routing, accessibility checks, analytics and technical updates.

Do not mirror District billing or personal data into the community website. Link to the authoritative service. Restrict member-only minutes or directories when required by policy. Confirm permissions and privacy before publishing names, photos, phone numbers, maps or meeting materials.

### Content freshness standards

- Show “Updated” dates for governing documents and key resource pages.
- Give every notice a posted date and an end date or review date.
- Remove expired emergency and wildlife alerts from the homepage.
- Review contact details quarterly and after board or staff changes.
- Reconfirm the dues amount, payment provider fees, support-fund labels and the payment recipient each dues cycle.
- Review payment records and reconcile successful payments against parcel number; keep ACH pending until confirmed.
- Review links and downloadable files at least twice a year.
- Provide a clear correction contact for factual errors.

The site should be findable for people searching for Blue Valley Acres, the Kremmling community, Grand County, the Blue River corridor, Land Share resources and new resident information. Avoid positioning the site as a real estate listing service unless that becomes an explicit scope.

### Suggested page titles

- Home: **Blue Valley Acres | Colorado Community Along the Blue River**
- About: **About Blue Valley Acres | Grand County, Colorado**
- New Here: **New Resident Guide | Blue Valley Acres**
- Resources: **Member Resources | Blue Valley Acres Land Share**
- Contact: **Contact Blue Valley Acres | Grand County, Colorado**
- Pay Dues: **Pay Annual Landowner Dues | Blue Valley Acres**

### Technical baseline

- Responsive, mobile-first layout; fast loading and compressed responsive photography.
- Semantic headings and landmarks; descriptive link names; keyboard access; visible focus indicators.
- WCAG 2.2 AA as the target, including color contrast, form labels, captions and reduced-motion support.
- Secure forms with a clear privacy notice and tested delivery routing.
- Wix payment setup requires a Wix plan that supports online payments and a connected provider. Confirm Stripe connection, supported payment methods, ACH settlement timing, receipt delivery and the payment destination in the actual site dashboard before launch.
- The dues checkout must preserve parcel number, owner name and email, payment method, dues, processing fee, optional fund and contribution amount, payment status and provider transaction ID for reconciliation. Never store raw card or bank credentials on the site.
- Redirect planning for any existing domain or Squarespace content that the organization controls. Confirm domain ownership before launch.
- Configure page titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, Open Graph images, XML sitemap, robots rules and useful 404 page.
- Add LocalBusiness or Organization structured data only when the entity type and address are appropriate; do not expose a private residential address.
- Use analytics with privacy-conscious settings and a documented purpose.
- Keep backups and a simple recovery procedure.

## 11. Design system starter specification

- **Content width:** 1160–1240 px maximum; narrower reading width of 680–760 px.
- **Grid:** 12 columns desktop; 6 tablet; 4 mobile, with generous gutters.
- **Spacing:** Base 8 px rhythm; larger section gaps of 72–112 px desktop and 48–72 px mobile.
- **Corner radius:** 6–12 px for cards and controls; avoid overly rounded app-like pills.
- **Buttons:** Solid River Slate primary; outlined Deep Spruce secondary; clear hover, focus and disabled states.
- **Cards:** Warm Stone or white surface, thin natural border, restrained shadow only when needed.
- **Motion:** 160–240 ms transitions; no motion essential to understanding; honor reduced motion.
- **Icons:** Simple line icons with consistent 1.5–2 px stroke; use labels for meaning.
- **Photography:** Full-bleed hero sparingly; consistent natural color and responsive crops.

2. Gather current governing documents, maps, notices and approved photography.
3. Approve the sitemap, copy direction and the facts listed in Section 12.
4. Design and build core pages with mobile and accessibility review.
5. Load only current approved resources and establish editor access.
6. Test all forms, document links, maps, external links, keyboard use and mobile layouts.

9. Review the site after 30 days and adjust based on real resident questions and search behavior.
